## Global style anchor (applies to every character sheet)

Every character prompt must carry this block verbatim so the cast reads as one film:

> Storybook 2D illustration, hand-painted gouache texture, soft rounded shapes,
> thick warm outlines, gentle Nordic autumn palette (burnt orange, ochre, moss green,
> slate blue, cream), soft diffuse lighting, no photorealism, no harsh shadows.
> Full-body character sheet on flat cream background, front / three-quarter / side views,
> neutral pose, consistent line weight.

**Proportion rule — do not apply one rule to the whole cast:**

- **Children (Alice) and creatures (Po, pandas):** large head, small rounded body — classic
  picture-book proportions.
- **Adults (Erik, villagers):** realistic adult proportions, ~8 head-lengths tall, small head,
  long legs with hips at the vertical midpoint. Never big-head cartoon proportions — that was
  what made Erik read as a child through three revisions.

Audience is a young child. The dragons are genuinely fearsome in Acts 1–2 (that is the
story's engine) but never gory, bloody or horror-styled.

## 1. Alice — protagonist

**Story facts (canon):** Nine years old. Lives in Husk village, northern Norway. Has never
seen a dragon and desperately wants to. Runs *toward* danger while everyone runs home.
Negotiates (bribes Po with "extra juicy bamboo"), carries a sword she never actually uses,
and resolves the whole conflict by talking. Brave, curious, warm, a little cheeky.

**Design:** Nine-year-old girl, slight build, warm tan skin, long dark brown hair in a
single loose braid that whips around when she flies. Big dark eyes, permanently
raised eyebrows of curiosity. Wears a rust-orange knitted Nordic tunic-sweater with a
simple cream snowflake band, brown leggings, worn leather boots, and a moss-green
half-cloak that streams behind her in flight. Small wooden-handled sword in a plain
leather scabbard on her back — clearly too big for her, comically so.

**Silhouette cue:** braid + trailing cloak + oversized sword on back.

**Expressions to sheet:** wide-eyed wonder · determined chin-up · mid-fall terror ·
delighted grin.

---

## 2. Po — the flying panda forest spirit

**Story facts (canon):** A forest spirit the villagers feed at the harvest festival. All the
pandas have powers; Po's is **flying**. Extremely shy, scared very easily, "nothing like
the other forest spirits" — which is exactly why Alice likes him best. Motivated by bamboo.
Gives Alice his sword before the flight. Big enough for Alice to ride.

**Design:** Chubby giant panda about the size of a small pony. Cream-and-charcoal fur with
a soft moss-green moss-like patch across his shoulders marking him as a *spirit*, not an
animal. Two small translucent leaf-shaped spirit wings — glowing pale gold, more decorative
than functional (he flies by will, not flapping). Faintly glowing gold eyes and a
soft aura. Nervous body language: shoulders hunched, paws clutched together, ears back.
Always a stalk of bamboo tucked somewhere on him.

**Silhouette cue:** round panda + tiny glowing leaf-wings + bamboo stalk.

**Expressions to sheet:** terrified squint · reluctant sigh · tempted-by-bamboo delight ·
proud-of-Alice smile.

> ⚠️ **Note:** "Po" + panda reads as *Kung Fu Panda*. Fine for a private family video, but the
> design must be entirely original (no green eyes / brown vest / DreamWorks face). If this is
> ever posted publicly, consider renaming. Flagging, not blocking — your call.

## 3. Vulcan — the fire dragon, king of all dragons

**Story facts (canon):** King of all dragons. The story's fake villain — the whole "attack"
is his crew celebrating the harvest festival too. Catches falling Alice mid-air "in one
zooop" with his *hands*. Casually knocks her sword away without malice. Speaks like a
good-natured big guy: "Calm down, no need to point that pointy thing at me."
Immediately apologetic once he learns the villagers are frightened.

**Design:** Enormous but soft-edged dragon — rounded snout, no sharp teeth showing, warm
amber eyes with round pupils (round = friendly). Scales in molten orange-to-deep-crimson
gradient with ember-glow seams that pulse like cooling lava. A crown-like crest of upswept
horns, chipped and lived-in rather than regal. Broad leathery wings with a sunset-orange
membrane. **Hands, not claws** — five expressive fingers, big enough to cradle Alice.
Small permanent curls of friendly smoke from the nostrils, never a flame aimed at anyone.

**Silhouette cue:** crown of horns + huge cupped hands + ember seams.

**Expressions to sheet:** playful mid-chase grin · genuinely puzzled · gentle apology ·
delighted at making friends.

## 4. The Dragon Crew — five elemental dragons

**Story facts (canon):** Live in the cave near Husk village. Types named in the story:
**earth, water, stone, crystal, light** (plus fire = Vulcan). They chase Alice and Po as a
game, then become friends.

Same body plan as Vulcan at roughly two-thirds his size, so they read as a crew.
Differentiate by material and silhouette only:

| Dragon | Material / colour | Silhouette cue | Personality beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Terra** (earth) | Loamy brown hide, moss and small ferns growing along the spine | Stubby, heavy, thick-legged | Slow, kind, the big sibling |
| **Marin** (water) | Translucent blue-green, water flowing over scales, drips light | Sleek, eel-like, long ribbon fins | Playful, splashy show-off |
| **Basalt** (stone) | Grey slab-like plates, cracked granite | Blocky, angular, armoured | Silent, stoic, blinks slowly |
| **Prism** (crystal) | Faceted amethyst-and-quartz scales, refracts rainbows | Spiky, angular wings like shards | Vain, preens, catches the light |
| **Lumen** (light) | Pale gold, semi-transparent, glows from within | Slender, wispy, trailing motes | Fastest, giggly, hard to look at |

> Names are mine — the story doesn't name them. Say the word and I'll swap in names your
> niece picks, which would be a nicer touch.

## 5. Erik — the Watcher

**Story facts (canon):** Watchers guard Husk village and look out for dragons. One cries out
"The dragons are coming!" and triggers the panic.

**Design:** Young soldier, early twenties — the village's protector, not an old lookout.
Athletic but lean, fair skin with wind-burnt cheeks, short sandy-blond hair, no beard
(maybe the faintest stubble). Wears a moss-green padded gambeson under a light leather
chest-guard with brass studs, a rust-orange cloak clasped at one shoulder (ties him to
Alice's palette), tall boots, and a simple round iron helmet he mostly carries rather than
wears. Brass spyglass on a hip strap and a curved signal horn slung across his chest;
a spear on his back — carried, never pointed at anyone.

Earnest and dutiful rather than tough — he takes the job very seriously, which is exactly
why he panics first. Reads as "capable big brother."

**Silhouette cue:** cloak + horn across chest + spear on back.

**Expressions to sheet:** scanning the horizon through the spyglass · wide-eyed alarm
mid-shout · sheepish relief at the end.

---

## 6. Villagers & other forest spirits — background ensemble

**Villagers:** Farmers in the same warm-knit Nordic palette as Alice — cream, rust, ochre,
moss. Harvest baskets, wheat sheaves, wooden carts. Mixed ages, a few children Alice's size.
Kept loose and simple; they're crowd, not cast.

**Other pandas:** Same spirit-panda build as Po but *confident* — chests out, relaxed poses,
each with a different power hinted by its aura colour (blue = water, green = growth, etc.).
They exist mainly to make Po's shyness read as unusual.

## Settled decisions

1. **Alice** — generic storybook girl (not modelled on a real child). Outfit is a child's
   traditional Norwegian **bunad**: white blouse, green embroidered bodice, ankle-length
   black skirt with red-and-white folk trim, belt-pouch, buckled shoes, plus her rust-orange
   half-cloak and the oversized sword. Locked.
2. **"Po"** — name kept as the author wrote it; design is fully original.
3. **Crew names** — Terra / Marin / Basalt / Prism / Lumen accepted.
4. **Art style** — gouache storybook, as specified in the style anchor above. Locked.
5. **Erik** — the Watcher is a young soldier in his early twenties, not an old lookout.

## v2 revision notes (applied)

- **Erik** aged up from early twenties to ~30: adult face with square jaw and stubble,
  broader shoulders, settled confident stance. Outfit and palette unchanged.
- **Po and the pandas** are now cheerful by default — open smile, ears up, bright eyes,
  bouncy posture. His timidity is now a *scene-level* beat, not a baseline expression.
- **Vulcan and the crew** rebuilt as genuinely fearsome: slit pupils, visible fangs, long
  sharp horns, jagged spines, armoured scales, torn battle-worn wings. Still no blood or
  horror — imposing, not traumatising.

## Vulcan's two states

The story turns on the reveal that the terrifying dragon was only celebrating. He therefore
has two locked sheets sharing one identical body, and the switch is a *directing* choice:

| | Acts 1–2 (`vulcan-sheet-v2.png`) | Act 3 (`vulcan-friendly.png`) |
|---|---|---|
| Pupils | narrow vertical slits | large round amber |
| Mouth | snarl, fangs bared | closed warm smile |
| Posture | low prowling crouch | upright, head lowered to child height |
| Wings | spread wide and high | folded loose and low |
| Hands | clawed and curled | open, palm-up |
| Lava seams | harsh white-hot | soft ember-orange |

The switch happens the moment he says *"Calm down, no need to point that pointy thing at me."*
Everything before that beat uses the fierce sheet; everything after uses the gentle one.

## Production notes

- **Generate scenes, never from text alone.** Pass the relevant character sheet(s) plus
  `scale-sheet-v2.png` as reference images on every scene prompt, or the cast and the size
  relationships will drift shot to shot.
- **Adult humans must be generated fresh, not edited.** Edit mode inherits the source body
  and will silently re-impose child proportions — this cost three revision rounds on Erik.
- **Po's timidity is scene direction, not model design.** His sheet is serene by default;
  make him shy in the moment (ears back, half-hiding, leaning away) when the beat calls for it.
