# Alice in Dragonland — Beat Sheet v2 (3:00 cut)

Source: "Alice in Dragonland" by a fourth-grader, used with her family's permission. Cast locked — see `character-bible.md`.
**Target runtime: 3:00 exactly. 24 beats, ≈7.5 s average.**
Narration budget ≈ **400 words** at a 135 wpm read-aloud pace.

Dialogue marked **[verbatim]** is the author's own text and must not be reworded.

---

## The one rule that governs every visual choice

The story turns on a **misunderstanding**: the villagers think they are under attack; the
dragons think they are at a party. If the visuals show real destruction, the reveal collapses
and Vulcan becomes a liar. So:

> **The "attack" must be genuinely ambiguous.** Dragons swooping, roaring, wheeling in the
> sky, fire in the *air* and never at the ground. Nothing broken, nobody hurt. On rewatch a
> viewer should see obvious play — loops, chases, showing off. On first watch, with the
> villagers' panic carrying the fear, it reads as an assault.

The terror lives in the villagers' faces and the music, not in the damage.

| D3 | Expecting a fight, Alice takes out her sword: **[verbatim]** *"Aaah!"* | Vulcan's palm | Alice, Vulcan | 7 |
| D4 | **[verbatim]** *"Calm down, no need to point that pointy thing at me"* — he carelessly knocks the sword out of her hands. It falls away and is gone | Vulcan's palm | Alice, Vulcan | 8 |
| D5 | **[verbatim]** *"Are you going to hurt me?"* — *"No! Why would I do that?"* — *"because you're hurting my village."* | Vulcan's palm | Alice, Vulcan | 9 |
| D6 | **[verbatim]** *"We are? We're just having fun celebrating the harvest festival."* Cut to the crew — the same loops and rolls, now unmistakably a game | Sky | Vulcan, crew, Po | 9 |
| D7 | **[verbatim]** *"that must be a big misunderstanding… we must go back to the cave."* — **[verbatim]** *"no need to go back, I can introduce you to everyone and we can all be friends."* | Sky | Alice, Vulcan | 9 |

> ### ⚠️ The state switch happens inside D4
> Everything up to and including the catch uses `vulcan-sheet-v2.png` — slit pupils, snarl,
> wings high. From the moment he speaks, switch to `vulcan-friendly.png` — round amber
> pupils, closed smile, wings folded, head lowered.
>
> **Switch on the line, not before it.** The audience should realise he is kind at exactly
> the same instant Alice does. If his face softens while he is still diving, the surprise is
> spent.

**Direction note (D6):** This is the shot that makes the whole film re-scan. Same choreography
as C4, read completely differently now that we know.

## Resolved adaptation decisions

1. **Po stays in the story.** He dives after Alice (D1), arrives as Vulcan catches her (D2),
   and is present through the ending. He no longer vanishes at the fall.
2. **The sword is gone for good** (D4). Alice is disarmed and then wins the entire conflict by
   talking — the strongest thematic beat in the film.
3. **Erik gets a bookend** (E1) — first to lower his spear.
4. **The chase ends explicitly** (D2) — the crew pulls up short and hovers, confused, which
   also explains why nobody is chasing Po anymore.

## What the 3:00 cut costs

Honest accounting of what the trim from 7:00 gives up, in case any of it matters to you:

- **The six dragon types** are now one narrated line over a montage (A2) rather than a
  moment each. Crystal, light and water get no individual screen time until the chase.
- **The harvest festival** is established in the opening shot rather than built up, so the
  world feels less lived-in before the alarm.
- **Act 3 dialogue is compressed** from eleven beats to seven; the back-and-forth between
  Alice and Vulcan moves faster than a real conversation would.
- **Sequence E is tight.** Twenty seconds is enough to land the ending, but not enough to
  savour it. If you later want one thing back, make it five more seconds here.

Nothing verbatim from the author was cut — every line of her dialogue survives.
