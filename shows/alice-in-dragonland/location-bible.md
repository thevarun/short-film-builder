# Alice in Dragonland — Location Bible v1

Companion to `character-bible.md` and `beat-sheet.md`.
Seven master plates in `art/locations/`. All 1376×768, 16:9.

**Rule:** every shot is generated with its location plate passed as a reference image,
exactly as characters are generated against their sheets. Plates are deliberately **empty of
characters** and composed with open space where figures go, so the same plate can serve
several shots at different framings.

## 1. Husk village & festival green

Timber houses with turf roofs, dark red and ochre walls, ringed by a low log palisade.
Harvest festival in progress: trestle tables, baskets, wheat sheaves, rust-and-cream bunting.
Golden late-afternoon light, autumn birches, fields in stripes, mountains behind, fjord left.

**Reads as:** warm, safe, communal, lived-in. This is what is at stake.
**Returns in Sequence E** at golden hour — same plate, warmer grade, now full of dragons too.

## 2. The watchtower

Weathered timber tower on the palisade, ladder up one side, shingled platform, brass signal
horn on the rail, spear rack, a folded blanket and clay cup on the floor. Fields, forest and
the dragon mountains beyond. Upper half of frame is open sky.

**Reads as:** watchful, exposed, a little lonely — the one place facing outward.
The blanket and cup do the character work for Erik before he says a word.

## 3. The forest spirit grove

Circular clearing in autumn birch and pine, light shafting through the canopy, a low mossy
offering stone with harvest baskets and bamboo laid across it, deep moss, ferns, drifting
pale-gold spirit lights, a footpath back toward the village.

**Reads as:** hushed, sacred, magical but never spooky. Kind.
Carries the most screen time in Sequence B — the bargain plays out entirely here.

## 4. The dragon cave

Vast cracked-stone mouth in a grey-blue mountainside, jagged rock teeth, ember-orange glow
deep inside, smoke wisps, scorched and claw-gouged rock, scree ledge. Far below in the
distance: the village, tiny and warm.

**Reads as:** awe and unease, not horror. The village in the same frame is the point — the
danger sits right on top of home.

## 5. Low golden sky

Low horizon, mostly warm open sky, big cumulus gold on top and violet underneath, autumn
forest and fields seen from above, a river catching light. Centre left open.

**Reads as:** exhilaration. The feeling of leaving the ground for the first time.

## 6. Sky over the village

Steep downward view. Village intact in the lower two-thirds, festival abandoned mid-
celebration — baskets tipped, tables empty — but **nothing broken and nothing burning**.
Smoke only high in the air. Upper third open for wheeling dragons.

> **This plate enforces the film's governing rule.** The "attack" causes no damage. All the
> fear is carried by the abandonment, the villagers' faces and the score. If a later shot
> ever puts fire on a roof, the Act 3 reveal collapses and Vulcan becomes a liar.

## 7. High thin sky

Extreme altitude. Deep cold blue above, icy cyan, white-gold horizon band, an unbroken floor
of cloud tops far below with one small gap showing land impossibly far down. Centre wide open
with a strong vertical drop.

**Reads as:** beautiful and terrifying. Deliberately cold and clean against the warm golden
sky lower down — the temperature shift *is* the danger signal.

## Palette and light progression

The film's colour temperature is a narrative device, not decoration. Do not break it:

| Sequence | Register | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| A — Village | Warm gold, saturated | Safe |
| B — Alarm | Warm but flattening | Safe cracking |
| C — Flight → chase | Gold → cool grey-blue | Leaving safety |
| D — Fall → truth | Cold blue, then warming on the reveal | Danger → understanding |
| E — Reunion | Warmest gold of the film | Safe again, but larger |

Sequence D is the whole arc in miniature: it *starts* at the coldest point in the film and
warms as Vulcan softens. Let the light change with him, on the same beat as the model switch
in D4.

## Known gaps

- **No interior plates.** No shot in the 3:00 cut goes indoors, so none were made. If a beat
  ever moves inside a house, a new plate is needed.
- **Plate 4 (cave) is used in exactly one beat** (A2) for about three seconds. Kept because
  it establishes where the dragons live and pays off in D10's *"we must go back to the cave."*
  If runtime gets tight again, this is the first plate to cut.
- **Plates 5 and 6 overlap** in C1. Use 5 for the wonder half of the beat and 6 for the
  disappointment half — the temperature drop between them is the emotional turn.
